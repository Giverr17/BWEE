

BLACK WOMEN’S ENTERPRISE EXCHANGE (BWEE)
Building Capital, Strategy, and Transnational Economic Power
A Research-Informed, Dual-Language Initiative Hosted at the Harriet Tubman Institute, York University
ABOUT BWEE
The Black Women’s Enterprise Exchange (BWEE) is a premier Canadian community, research-informed, and networking platform dedicated to the economic advancement, leadership development, skills acquisition, and enterprise scaling of Black women, young women, and youth across Canada. 
Grounded in doctoral and postdoctoral research on Black women entrepreneurs in Toronto and across international diaspora contexts, BWEE addresses deep-seated systemic economic barriers to turn individual resilience into collective economic power. Hosted at the Harriet Tubman Institute for Research on Africa and Its Diaspora at York University, BWEE serves as an institutional home for Black female entrepreneurs, small business owners, professionals, emerging youth leaders, students, scholars, and community changemakers. 
We are explicitly committed to Equity, Diversity, and Inclusion (EDI) and Anti-Racism. Our platform provides tailored structural support for Black women and youth with disabilities, and ensures full inclusion and advocacy for Black LGBTQ+ women and youth navigating the small business landscape. 
OUR VISION
To build an equitable, inclusive, anti-racist, and globally connected economic ecosystem where Black women, young women, students, and youth across Canada, including newcomers, immigrant women, individuals with disabilities, and LGBTQ+ youth—transition from necessity-driven survival into strategic growth, early entrepreneurial readiness, long-term wealth creation, and global economic leadership. 
OUR MISSION
BWEE equips Black women entrepreneurs, small business owners, young women, and students across Canada with actionable business intelligence, access to non-dilutive capital, strategic executive mentorship, practical vocational training, cutting-edge AI and digital skills, and cross-border networks. 
Grounded in anti-racist principles and dual-language (English–French) accessibility, we foster an early entrepreneurial mindset in youth, rapidly integrate newcomer and immigrant women into the Canadian economic system, drive policy reform through academic research, and build lasting financial independence across generations. 
CORE PILLARS &amp; SUPPORT AREAS
1. Practical Vocational Trades &amp; Youth Enterprise Pathways
Hands-on vocational skills acquisition—including hairstyling, makeup artistry, nail technology, catering, culinary arts, and service trades. 
Youth &amp; Student Skill Acceleration: Tailored vocational bootcamps for young women and students to introduce enterprise concepts early, build practical craft mastery, and foster an independent entrepreneurial mindset before or alongside post-secondary education. 
Targeted workforce integration strategies derived from Toronto postdoctoral research to help newcomer women and young entrepreneurs establish licensed, high-revenue small businesses. 
2. AI, Technology &amp; Digital Enterprise Readiness
Practical training in Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools, automation, digital marketing, e-commerce, and software for small business efficiency. 
Next-Gen Digital Skills: Equipping young women, students, and youth with AI skills and digital literacy to launch tech-enabled businesses, streamline operations, create content, and remain globally competitive in modern digital markets. 
3. EDI, Anti-Racism &amp; Intersectional Equity
Embedded structural advocacy and anti-racist frameworks across all programs. 
Tailored accessibility accommodations and targeted business incubation for Black women and youth with disabilities. 
Safe, inclusive, and affirmative spaces for Black LGBTQ+ small business owners, young entrepreneurs, and youth founders. 
4. Capital Access, Grants &amp; Financial Literacy
Expert guidance on navigating federal, provincial, and foundation grants. 
Training in proposal writing, pitch presentation, investor readiness, and youth entrepreneurship funding calls. 
Community-backed alternative financing models and financial literacy to end over-reliance on personal debt. 
5. Strategic Enterprise Scaling &amp; Operations
Transitioning micro-enterprises past the critical 5-year survival threshold. 
Implementation of standard operating procedures, team delegation, digital systems, and strategic management to build scalable assets. 
6. Dual-Language Accessibility (English–French)
Strategic commitment to bilingual inclusion to eliminate linguistic isolation. 
Full integration of Francophone Black women, youth, and students in Quebec and official language minority communities across Canada into national business networks. 
7. Research-Backed Policy Advocacy &amp; Diaspora Connections
Translating academic research into actionable policy recommendations for Canadian financial institutions, educational bodies, and government agencies. 
Leveraging global diaspora networks across Africa, Europe, the Caribbean, and the Americas for international trade, youth exchange, market expansion, and cross-border mentorship. 
INAUGURAL LAUNCH MILESTONE
On August 25, 2026, BWEE officially introduced its vision during its global virtual launch event themed "From Survival to Strategy: Building, Scaling, and Sustaining Black Women-Owned Businesses". 
Hosted in partnership with the Harriet Tubman Institute at York University and the School of Women in Leadership &amp; Business, the event convened 172 registered participants representing 62 Canadian registrants across 8 provinces and international delegates across 13 confirmed countries. 
Featuring keynote addresses from academic leaders, cultural icons, and media executives; including Dr. Salewa Olawoye (Director, Harriet Tubman Institute), Prof. Peju Layiwola (Founder, WYAF), and Dr. Nikki Clarke (Founder, The Nikki Clarke Network), the launch established BWEE as a vital force for economic equity, youth empowerment, anti-racism, and bilingual inclusion in Canada. 
FUTURE STRATEGIC ROADMAP
Youth &amp; Student Vocational Trades Bootcamps: Practical workshops in beauty, esthetics, catering, and creative trades designed to give young women and students hands-on business skills and an entrepreneurial jumpstart. 
AI &amp; Digital Future-of-Work Labs: Interactive AI and digital tool training specifically tailored for young entrepreneurs, students, and business owners looking to automate and scale operations. 
Accessible Youth Business Incubators: Specialized business hubs built with full accessibility features for young entrepreneurs with disabilities and inclusive spaces for LGBTQ+ youth founders. 
Grant Navigation &amp; Investment Pitch Clinics: Hands-on training to help business owners and young founders secure non-dilutive government, corporate, and youth enterprise funding. 
National Intergenerational Mentorship Network: Pairing established corporate leaders and seasoned business owners with emerging young women and student entrepreneurs across Canada. 
Bilingual Resource Hub: Expanding translated toolkits, research briefs, and dual-language community forums for Francophone and Anglophone network members.
